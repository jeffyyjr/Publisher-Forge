# Publisher Forge — Internal Deal Terms

Updated: 2026-09-27

Internal seller material. Do not send this file to buyers.

## Price

**Published asking price:** $19,500 USD.

**Internal initial valuation range:** $15,000–$22,500.

**Private cash-equivalent floor:** $10,000.

The private floor is not a buyer-facing number and should not be volunteered in outreach or early negotiation.

## Preferred transaction

Prefer a straightforward asset/IP sale with:

- clear cash consideration;
- payment secured/confirmed before final control transfer;
- buyer-controlled destination repository and hosting;
- fresh buyer-owned credentials;
- written asset schedule;
- existing production user data excluded by default;
- no seller financing unless the economics materially compensate for added risk;
- no vague earn-out counted at face value when comparing against a cash offer.

## Price interpretation

### $18,000–$22,500
Strong current-stage outcome. Favor a clean closing and limited transition burden.

### $15,000–$18,000
Reasonable negotiation zone if diligence is simple and payment terms are clean.

### $10,000–$15,000
Consider only after weighing speed, certainty, support burden, included assets and payment structure.

### Below $10,000
Below the current private floor unless the buyer provides exceptional and quantifiable additional consideration.

## Offer comparison

Compare offers on **cash-equivalent value**, not headline value.

Discount:
- speculative earn-outs;
- future-revenue promises;
- conditional payments;
- seller-financed balances;
- equity that cannot be readily valued or sold.

Also account for:
- transition/support hours;
- data-migration obligations;
- custom-development requests;
- exclusivity periods;
- delayed closing;
- platform/account transfer complexity.

## Asset schedule default

Include:

- Publisher Forge source repository and history.
- Repository-owned Publisher Forge code/IP.
- Repository-owned documentation.
- Sale-package documentation.
- Deployment/configuration documentation.
- Repository-owned brand/product assets that are specifically listed.
- Approved verified demo media once completed.
- Agreed reasonable handoff assistance.

Exclude unless separately and expressly added:

- production SQLite/user data;
- personal credentials or API keys;
- personal Google/Gmail;
- seller banking/payment accounts;
- unrelated repositories including Oracle Stack / Lead-OS;
- unrelated social/community accounts;
- third-party assets beyond their license rights;
- domains or external accounts that are not specifically listed.

## Transition default

Offer documentation-led handoff and reasonable Q&A necessary to get the buyer-controlled deployment running.

Do not silently include:
- indefinite support;
- new feature development;
- buyer-specific integrations;
- ongoing operational management;
- guaranteed marketplace approval;
- guaranteed provider/platform compatibility;
- guaranteed revenue or customer acquisition.

Any post-close development should be a separate paid engagement if both parties want it.

## Clean closing sequence

1. Agree headline price and transaction structure.
2. Sign the asset purchase / assignment documents.
3. Confirm payment/escrow mechanics.
4. Freeze the final asset schedule.
5. Buyer creates fresh credentials and destination accounts.
6. Transfer/copy repository to buyer control.
7. Deploy buyer-controlled production environment.
8. Run tests/security gate and health checks.
9. Verify signup/sign-in, Trend Radar, production and packaging paths.
10. Transfer approved brand/media assets.
11. Rotate/revoke seller-side access.
12. Complete agreed transition window.
13. Record written acceptance / closeout.

## Negotiation guardrails

Do not overclaim traction to defend price. The strongest price argument is the amount of working product and documented engineering a buyer avoids rebuilding.

Do not disclose the internal floor simply because a buyer asks for the “lowest” number.

Do not transfer source control, production control or credentials before the transaction mechanics protect payment.

Do not allow production-user data to become an assumed asset.

Do not promise future development in the base sale price unless it is explicitly scoped and valued.

## When to reconsider the asking price upward

Reprice only when supported by new evidence such as:

- paying customers;
- recurring revenue;
- attributable activation/retention;
- repeat independent feature use;
- completed billing with successful transactions;
- a transferable audience/domain asset with demonstrable value.

## When to reconsider downward

Revisit valuation if diligence uncovers:

- unresolved security issues;
- material licensing uncertainty;
- broken core production paths;
- major data-transfer complications;
- meaningful rebuild work required for the core product.
